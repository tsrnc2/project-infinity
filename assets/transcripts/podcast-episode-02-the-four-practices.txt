The Four Practices

Elder Rowan: Today we name the four practices: witness, refine, create, and serve.

Maya Vale: They sound simple, but each one asks for a different kind of courage.

Elder Rowan: Witness asks for honest sight. It says, begin with what is real. Do not decorate the wound, and do not worship it.

Maya Vale: Refine asks for disciplined change. Not a dramatic vow, but a repeated action that can be measured.

Elder Rowan: Creation asks the changed life to make something visible. A repaired room. A written apology. A meal. A lesson. A tool. A song.

Maya Vale: And service asks whether my growth reduces harm beyond myself.

Elder Rowan: Correct. Transformation that never leaves the mirror is incomplete. The will grows stronger when it protects, repairs, builds, and gives.

Maya Vale: But service must not become control. The best help strengthens another person ability to help themselves.

Elder Rowan: Consent is sacred. You may offer a hand. You may not own the path. You may lead someone to the waters of knowledge, but only they can drink.

Maya Vale: So today I can ask: what do I witness, what do I refine, what do I create, and who is helped by the change?

Elder Rowan: Write one answer for each practice. Keep them plain. Keep them possible. Keep them honest.

Maya Vale: I witness without disguise. I refine without cruelty. I create from what changes me. I serve without ownership.

Elder Rowan: This is the daily wheel. Turn it once, and the day has a center.
