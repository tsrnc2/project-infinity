The Turning Life

Dialogues with Father Rowan, an elder priest with a slow British-Boston cadence, and adult partners Maya Vale and Amara Keene as they ask about recovery, everyday life, love, consent, and faithful relationship.

The show

Cast and conversation format

Episodes are built as sectioned table conversations: opening, personal life check-in, teaching, emotional turn, daily practice, and closing statement. Maya and Amara are West Coast adult partners who speak from recovery and ordinary daily life, and Father Rowan answers slowly as a religious priest. Each episode uses dry source dialogue, separate transition music and ambience stems, and a master-track manifest so sound effects can be changed without altering the source tracks.

Maya Vale

A red-haired West Coast adult cohost in Tibetan traditional clothing. Maya is in recovery from compulsive sexual behavior and asks direct questions about honesty, desire, tenderness, chores, work, and daily practice inside love.

Father Rowan

An older religious priest voiced in a slow British-Boston-inspired cadence. He answers with calm counsel, consent, and spiritual discipline.

Amara Keene

A brunette West Coast adult cohost in Native Hawaiian ceremonial-inspired attire, and Maya's romantic partner. Amara is in recovery from fentanyl addiction and presses for practical answers, boundaries, grounded care, and how recovery survives ordinary days.

Production Covenant

  • Episodes should not pressure listeners to join, give money, or isolate from outside support.
  • Stories from members require consent and may be anonymized.
  • Each episode opens with an original geometric rhythm intro built from four, seven, and twelve-count cycles.
  • Each episode includes a short emotional close that names fear, tenderness, grief, or relief without melodrama.
  • Audio should feel like a careful home table recording: clear speech, modest room texture, and no over-polished studio sheen.
  • Teachings should stay close to everyday life: work, chores, recovery, money stress, conflict, rest, meals, errands, family, and ordinary choices.
  • Teachings should end with a concrete practice, not vague inspiration.
  • Each episode uses distinct sections, a personal life check-in, natural conversation hesitations, one integrated emotional moment, subtle park ambience, original free-use transition stingers, non-destructive master-track manifests, and a consistent closing statement.
  • Corrections and concerns should be invited publicly.
