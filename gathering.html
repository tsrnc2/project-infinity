Annual gathering

The Turning Games

Once each year, circles gather for witness, service, sacred play, shared meals, and games that turn practice into visible skill. Prizes honor effort, teamwork, repair, and usefulness.

View the Games Prize Covenant

Purpose

Sacred play with clear boundaries

The annual gathering is not a test of worth. It is a public practice field where members can learn, laugh, serve, repair, and celebrate change without coercion or status games.

Witness

See the year honestly

Members name one transformation, one failure, one repair, and one lesson that can help the next circle.

Practice

Turn belief into skill

Games are built around memory, cooperation, symbols, consent, service, geometry, and practical problem solving.

Service

Leave usefulness behind

Every gathering closes with a service project, a transparent ledger, and a plan for reducing harm after the event.

Annual rhythm

Three days of turning

Day 1

Arrival and witness

Opening meal, consent agreements, circle introductions, calendar reading, and the Lantern Walk of kept promises.

Day 2

Games and shared feast

Team games, symbol challenges, planet walk, geometry build, youth-safe activities, prize ceremony, music, and storytelling.

Day 3

Service and repair

Community service project, prize ledger review, repair requests, closing vows, the all-member leader vote, and council appointments for the next year.

Yearly leadership vote

During the annual gathering, all members vote on one leader for the coming year. After the vote is recorded, the elected leader appoints the council by service role. Appointed council members must accept their duties publicly and remain reviewable by the membership.

Games

The annual games

Games should reward patience, attention, courage, cooperation, and ethical imagination. Winning matters less than becoming easier to trust while playing.

Symbols

Calendar Quest

Teams read a date through daily, weekly, monthly, yearly, moon, and birth-moon symbols, then choose one concrete practice from the reading.

Planets

Pinwheel Planet Walk

Players move through a floor map of the solar pinwheel, matching planets to virtues such as witness, relation, structure, service, and humility.

Practice

Four Practices Relay

Teams complete witness, refinement, creation, and service stations. Judges score clarity, consent, usefulness, and teamwork.

Geometry

Sacred Build

Circles create a temporary altar, seal, or pattern from simple materials, then explain the number, promise, and service behind it.

Care

Consent Council Challenge

Teams practice asking, refusing, negotiating, and repairing boundaries in everyday situations without pressure or shame.

Service

Kindness Ledger

Participants collect verified acts of help during the gathering: setup, cleanup, translation, rides, accessibility, listening, and repair.

Prizes

Prizes that do not buy status

Prizes are symbolic, useful, or service-directed. They never buy rank, forgiveness, leadership, spiritual worth, or special access to the community.

Lantern

Lantern of Witness

A handmade lamp, journal, or reading cloth awarded for honest self-examination and clean repair.

Seal

Golden Seal of Creation

A symbolic seal, art print, or altar piece awarded for useful sacred design, music, writing, craft, or teaching.

Seed

Service Seed Prize

Supplies or transparent funding for a small service project proposed by a team and reviewed by the circle ledger.

Circle

Circle Banner

A traveling banner awarded to the group that best models cooperation, consent, accessibility, and shared responsibility.

Fair-play covenant

  • No gambling, buy-ins, raffles of meaningful value, or pay-to-win prizes.
  • Rules, judges, scoring, and prize sources are announced before games begin.
  • Children and vulnerable participants require age-appropriate games, supervision, and privacy protections.
  • Every game has a noncompetitive way to participate through service, setup, accessibility, or witness.
  • Prizes can be declined, shared, redirected to service, or replaced with a non-material honor.
  • Disputes are handled through calm review, not public shame.

Prepare

What to bring

Bring a journal, comfortable clothing, a reusable cup, any accessibility needs, and one story of transformation you are willing to witness without forcing anyone else to carry it.

Accessibility and care

  • Quiet spaces should be available during loud games and ceremonies.
  • Food labels, sober spaces, and transportation needs should be planned before the gathering.
  • Participation in testimony, games, touch, photos, and public recognition is always optional.
  • The gathering does not replace medical, legal, financial, or emergency support.
